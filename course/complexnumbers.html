    Section Sums and Products

    Definition 8. Complex Numbers.

    \begin{equation*} \mathbb{C} = \{(x,y):x,y\in\mathbb{R}\}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Complex numbers of the form \((x,0)\) correspond to points on the real axis. Complex numbers of the form \((0,y)\) correspond to points on the imaginary axis and are called pure imaginary numbers.
    🔗

    Definition 15. Additive and Multiplicative Identities.

    The additive identity \(0=(0,0)\) and the multiplicative identity \(1=(1,0)\) for \(\mathbb{R}\) carry over to \(\mathbb{C}\text{.}\) That is, \(z+0=z\) and \(z\cdot 1=z\) for every \(z\in\mathbb{C}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 16.

    What is the multiplicative inverse of the complex number \(z=3+4i=(3,4)\text{?}\) Is it unique?
    🔗
    Hint. If \((u,v)\) is such an inverse, then
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} (3,4)(u,v)=(1,0). \end{equation*}
    This fact gives two equations in the two unknowns \(u\) and \(v\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Section Further Algebraic Properties

    Definition 21. Division.

    For \(z_2 \neq 0\text{,}\) we define
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{z_1}{z_2} = z_1 z_2^{-1}, \end{equation*}
    where \(z_2^{-1}\) is the multiplicative inverse of \(z_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 22.

    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \frac{2-i}{3+4i} = (a,b) \end{equation*}
    for some real numbers \(a\) and \(b\text{.}\) Determine these values.
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 23.

    Let \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) be complex numbers.
    🔗
    1. Write \((z_1+z_2)^4\) as a linear combination of powers of \(z_1\) and \(z_2\text{.}\)
      🔗
    2. Prove by mathematical induction that
      🔗
    \begin{equation*} (z_1+z_2)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \frac{n!} {k!(n-k)!} z_1^{\,n-k} z_2^{\,k}, \end{equation*}
    for every integer \(n\ge 1\text{.}\)

    Section Vectors and Moduli

    With each nonzero complex number \(z=x+iy\) we can associate a vector.
    🔗
    Addition of complex numbers is the same as vector addition. That is, \(z_1+z_2\) is the vector that forms the diagonal of the parallelogram determined by \(z_1\) and \(z_2\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Definition 26. Modulus.

    The modulus of a complex number \(z=x+iy\) is
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \|z\| = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    • \(\|z\|\) is the length of the vector representing \(z\text{.}\)
      🔗
    • If \(z\) is real, then \(\|z\|\) is simply its absolute value.
      🔗
    • While \(z_1<z_2\) has no meaning, \(\|z_1\|<\|z_2\|\) does.
      🔗

    Checkpoint 27.

    Compute \(\|3+4i\|\text{,}\) \(\|3-4i\|\text{,}\) and \(\|-4-3i\|\text{.}\) What does this tell you about the relationship among these three complex numbers?
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 28. Distance.

    The distance between two complex numbers \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) is
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \|z_1-z_2\|. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Checkpoint 33.

    Geometrically describe the set
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \left\{ z\in\mathbb{C}: \|z-1\|=\|z+i\| \right\}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    From the diagram, we have
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} |\operatorname{Re}(z)| \le \|z\|, \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} |\operatorname{Im}(z)| \le \|z\|, \end{equation*}
    and
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \|z\|^2 = (\operatorname{Re}(z))^2 + (\operatorname{Im}(z))^2. \end{equation*}

    Section Complex Conjugates

    Definition 35. Complex Conjugate.

    The complex conjugate of \(z=x+iy\) is
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \overline{z}=x-iy. \end{equation*}
    Geometrically, \(\overline{z}\) is the reflection of \(z\) across the real axis.
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 36.

    Prove any three of the following statements.
    🔗
    1. \(\displaystyle \overline{\overline{z}}=z\)
      🔗
    2. \(\displaystyle \|\overline{z}\|=\|z\|\)
      🔗
    3. \(\displaystyle \overline{z_1+z_2}=\overline{z_1}+\overline{z_2}\)
      🔗
    4. \(\displaystyle \overline{z_1-z_2}=\overline{z_1}-\overline{z_2}\)
      🔗
    5. \(\displaystyle \overline{z_1z_2}=\overline{z_1}\,\overline{z_2}\)
      🔗
    6. \(\displaystyle \overline{\frac{z_1}{z_2}} =\frac{\overline{z_1}}{\overline{z_2}}\)
      🔗
    7. \(\displaystyle z+\overline z=2\operatorname{Re}(z)\)
      🔗
    8. \(\displaystyle z\overline z=\|z\|^2\)
      🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 37.

    Sketch the set of points satisfying \(\operatorname{Re}(\overline z-i)=2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Checkpoint 38.

    When \(z_2\) and \(z_3\) are nonzero, show that
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \overline{\left( \frac{z_1}{z_2z_3} \right)} = \frac{\overline{z_1}} {\overline{z_2}\,\overline{z_3}} \end{equation*}
    and
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \left\| \frac{z_1}{z_2z_3} \right\| = \frac{\|z_1\|} {\|z_2\|\|z_3\|}. \end{equation*}
    🔗

    Checkpoint 39.

    1. Prove by induction that conjugation distributes over finite sums and products.
